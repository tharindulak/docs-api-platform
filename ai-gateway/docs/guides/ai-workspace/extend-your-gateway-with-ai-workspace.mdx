:::note[Requires AI Workspace]
Everything on this page needs a running AI Workspace deployment. The gateway serves traffic without one.
:::

The gateway serves traffic on its own. Connecting it to [AI Workspace](docs:ai-workspace/cloud/), the control plane for AI traffic across your organization, adds central management across every gateway you run.

## What connecting the gateway adds

- **One console across gateways.** AI Workspace manages [LLM providers](docs:ai-workspace/cloud/guides/llm-providers/overview/), App LLM proxies, MCP proxies, and the credentials behind them for every gateway you connect.
- **Central configuration.** Configure an artifact in the console, then [deploy it to one gateway or several](docs:ai-workspace/cloud/guides/llm-proxies/configure-proxy/).
- **Artifacts in both directions.** Artifacts you create through the gateway management API [sync upward automatically](docs:ai-workspace/cloud/guides/sync-gateway-created-artifacts/) and appear in the console as copies the gateway owns.
- **Policies in one place.** Attach [guardrails, rate limits, and traffic controls](docs:ai-workspace/cloud/guides/policies/overview/) to providers and proxies from the console.
- **Traffic insights.** Review [token usage, latency, cost, and guardrail events](docs:ai-workspace/cloud/guides/insights/) across your applications and consumers.

Connecting a gateway doesn't make it depend on the control plane. If AI Workspace is unreachable, the gateway carries on and the sync catches up once the connection is restored.

## Try it locally

You don't need a hosted deployment to decide whether this suits you. [Get started with AI Workspace](docs:ai-workspace/cloud/get-started/getting-started/) runs the control plane on your own machine, then walks you through creating a gateway and configuring a provider.
